# UI design

Screen/shell design. Modules [components.md](components.md), requirements [requirements.md](requirements.md).

## App shell

- **Destinations per the bar** in Material 3 `NavigationBar` (NFR-1): Recipes, Ingredients and Shopping on an owned bar, the first two on a guest one (FR-BAR-4). The stack is indexed by position in the offered list, not the enum's index, so the two cannot drift on a bar that offers fewer ([components.md](components.md#state-contracts)). Two is below Material's three to five; accepted over a destination that only says it is empty. Settings: app bar gear (amounts, vocabularies, data exchange, [switching bars](#bars)).
- **`IndexedStack`** (keeps scroll/search). Forms/settings pushed via `Navigator`. Recipe view inside list. `Navigator` 1.0, no nested routers. Each destination is told whether it is on show: staying alive is free for two of three, not for the [shopping screen](#shopping-screen).
- **Theme**: seed colour, platform-picked light/dark. `dimmedInk` (`onSurfaceVariant` at 60%) is the one dim: a hint, so empty ≠ filled, and an ingredient a group offers that the bar lacks. One home, so the two cannot drift.
- **A name reaches its own row on another destination** (FR-DIS-9, [ADR 19](adr/19-a-destination-sends-the-reader-to-another.md)): one tap, on a basket's recipes and ingredients and on the ingredients a recipe line names. **Nothing marks a name as a way out**: the ripple is the whole of it, as with the ingredients row's own tap (FR-ING-2). A per-row arrow was refused: the trailing slot already holds tag and stock dots, and a widget repeated down every row is clutter. So a run whose names lead somewhere looks like any other, which is accepted. Recipe cards' inert parts are a measure and a mark, not a name.
- **The reader is put where they asked, not told why they cannot.** The serving screen clears every narrowing (its own tag and base picks, the list's search and order) and opens the row alone, as the [random pick](#recipes-screen) does, wash included. Only a jump clears a search. An edit leaves one standing, since the reader is working *within* the query; a jump names a row outside it.
- **Back undoes a jump**, one at a time, and leaves the app once none remain. A bottom-bar tap clears the way back: a chosen destination has nothing to return *from*. Back restores their place, not their narrowings: a basket is found open, a cleared search stays cleared.
- **The startup load is met once, here.** While it runs the app is a **bare spinner** with no app bar or bottom bar, since chrome naming an unread bar would say more than the app knows. A failed load fills the screen with its message. Only past both is anything built, so every screen below reads a collection ([components.md](components.md#state-contracts)). One gate, not a wrapper per screen.
- **`EmptyState`**: icon, title, fill hint.
- **A card's trailing stands as far from the edge as the text is from the other**: a ⋮'s glyph box (dots centred) and a chip with no menu share that inset. A chip beside a ⋮ sits one inset clear of the glyph box, leaving the floating button more of the card. The button still reaches a fingertip across, into the insets either side, which hold nothing else to tap.
- **`LoadIssues`**: shows FR-DAT-4 problems above all screens, from startup or a crossing alike. Dismissing clears only the issues on show, so a later bar's trouble is not swallowed by an earlier tap.

## Bars

Behind the gear, **Change bar** is a travelling row and the **last** one on the Settings list. Not in the app bar, a drawer or a fourth destination: switching is rarer than reaching a recipe, so it sits with the non-destinations and the bottom bar keeps its meaning. It is last as the way *out* of this bar; the rows above act on the bar in hand. No ellipsis: the chevron already says a screen follows.

- **The app opens on the bar it was left in**: `openId` outlives the run ([ADR 20](adr/20-the-app-holds-many-bars.md)). **No bar open means this screen is home**: with no destination to draw, the app opens on the list, whose empty state says what a bar is. That covers a first run and a reader who deleted their open bar. Deleting it leaves them on the list they are on, since choosing a next bar would be arbitrary.
- **One card a bar, its counts always in view.** The counts sit on the record (ADR 20), so nothing folds behind a tap: name, how current and holdings all stand at once. The bar list also manages bars, so no second place holds half of that. **A card shows counts, never contents**: the list reads the index alone, so every card is free to draw.
- **Sharing joins Rename and Delete behind the row's ⋮**, opening the room ([Sharing](#sharing)) on *that* bar, not the one in hand. Sharing a bar one is not standing in is why this second door exists (FR-BAR-6). A card already carrying two bar-level actions is where a third belongs. **A shared bar says so on the card**, beside how current it is: an offer is a standing arrangement a reader may forget.
- **Rename and Delete sit behind that same ⋮**, the control the ingredients and tag lists use, reachable without any other act on the card: neither operation cares what the bar holds. **Rename is offered on a guest bar too**: what a bar is called on this device is the reader's, as the unit it reads in is (FR-BAR-3), and no refresh takes it back ([ADR 21](adr/21-the-file-carries-one-bar.md)). The owner's name is only where the field starts. An owned rename keeps the [new-bar form's name rule](#new-bar); a guest's is exempt.
- **The subtitle says how current, never ownership**: "Updated: 3 hours ago" for an owner's own edit, "Refreshed: 2 days ago" for a guest's last answer from its source (FR-BAR-5). Coarse on purpose: the question is whether to refresh, which seconds do not sharpen. A bar the device never dated shows nothing, since "just now" would lie about an index with no stamps. The colon makes the date the answer.
- **No card says which bar is loaded.** Cards count off the index ([ADR 20](adr/20-the-app-holds-many-bars.md)), so "loaded" would mark nothing a reader can act on; the open bar is the one they just came from.
- **Whose bar it is is a chip beside the ⋮**, "Owned" or "Guest", read as the ingredients row's stock chip is, on the same traffic light: **green** for owned, **amber** for guest (see [colours](#tag-and-stock-colours)). The light tracks what the app lets a reader do, not stock. A guest bar is the amber case, since everything that writes is missing from it (FR-BAR-4).
- **A tap on the card opens the bar, on every bar, the loaded one included.** No separate open button: counts show before the tap, so a second tap would add a step without a second look. A stray tap costs a return trip to Bars, accepted. The loaded bar's subtitle, not an absent control, says "you are here". Tapping it lands the reader on the recipes with no way back to the list: a chosen bar has nothing to return from.
- **The bar's name leads the title**: "Home bar's Recipes", "Anna's Ingredients". A bare destination name answers a smaller question once there are several bars. Only the bar's own destinations carry it; Settings, the vocabularies and the forms keep plain titles. Nothing else marks the bar: no subtitle, no strip.
- **A guest bar is told by the bottom bar's shape**, not a badge (FR-BAR-4): two destinations where an owned bar has three.
- **Everything that writes is absent on a guest, never refused** (FR-BAR-4). `barWriterProvider` answering null ([components.md](components.md#state-contracts)) drives every control: no add button on either list, no ⋮ on an ingredients row, no Edit or Delete on a recipe card, no stock rotation on a row tap. **Scale & convert survives**, reading the owner's line rather than changing it; so do search, the narrowings, the orders, the random pick and the jumps. A ⋮ with nothing left draws nothing: `RowMenu` builds only what the bar allows.
- **The Settings rows that would write dim rather than vanish**: Tags and Units, greyed whole and leading nowhere. A missing menu row reads as one the reader forgot; a dimmed one says what the app does with a bar of one's own. Amounts, Share and Change bar stay live: the pick is the reader's on any bar, a guest already holds what a file or the LAN would carry (FR-DAT-1, FR-BAR-6), and Change bar is the way out. **Import is replaced, not dimmed**: on a guest bar the file row reads **Refresh**, "ask its source for a newer copy" (FR-BAR-5). The same exchange from the other side: a guest bar does have business with a file.
- **Swipe down to refresh** (FR-BAR-5), on a guest bar's lists only. It is the standard "ask the source again" gesture, so the shell has no refresh control or as-of. Staleness reads on the card; on the bar itself, one gesture from current is worth more than the distance. **A bar holding nothing answers the pull too**: the empty state is given something to overscroll.
- **A refresh that did not land says so above the destinations**, beside the load banner and worded like it. Unreadable content gets the `line N:` issues (FR-DAT-4); an unreachable source gets one of three reasons, in the app's own words. Two banners, since a torn file and a silent source are different news. **Nothing is said while a refresh is out**: the spinner says it, and asking again clears the last result.

### New bar

**A pushed form, not a dialog**: name first, then where the contents come from, then what becomes of them. A dialog carries a name alone. A picked file must be *read* first (counts, refusals) and offers destinations to choose between (FR-BAR-7), neither fitting over a list. Save sits in the app bar like every commit; back asks before dropping typed input, as the recipe form does (`editor_form.dart`).

**It is the same screen [Import](#data) pushes.** One file, the same three questions (what to call it, what it holds, what mode it stands in), so the two cannot drift apart. Both start with nothing in hand and the same two roads. Founding's owner road is **Owned**, noted as starting empty; importing's is **Replace**, its name field starting on the bar it is bound for, with nothing to save until something arrives. The name field autofocuses only when there is typing to do: on the import entry a keyboard would argue with the bar pick.

- **The roads in are one segmented control**: **From import** for a file the reader hands over (FR-BAR-7) beside **From LAN** for one shared on the network (FR-BAR-8), spanning the width of the fields and the **Mode** choice so the form reads as one column. A tap leaves the screen, but **the road that brought what is in hand stays lit and ticked**, as a picked Mode segment does: the form holds that road's answer. Tapping the lit road takes it again, as its "Another" label offers. No heading; the note under the segments says what standing without one means.
- **Only the road taken changes its words**: **Another file** or **Another bar**; the other reads as before. Nobody is offered "another" of what they never picked, but a taken road stays takeable: the way out of an unreadable file, and the way to a better one. Terse because both share a row; half a phone fits an icon and about twelve characters.
- **The way back to what the screen opened as is Reset, in the app bar beside Save**, before Save as the lesser act. A way back is not a third road, so it stays out of the road choice. Offered only where something is in hand, on either entry: it restores the opening name and shows the note for an empty form.
- **A fetched bar is waited for where its contents will land.** The roads close and the spinner draws in the Contents cards' spot, so no second tap can start a second fetch. It is the dialog's spinner, and as short as the network allows: a fetch stops at the wanted device, not at the window's end.
- **A bar nearby is picked in a dialog and fetched afterwards.** The dialog browses once on opening: each bar is followed by its device after a centred, dimmed dot, the separator every one-line summary uses. The picked bar is ringed as a picked tag chip is; **Select** closes the dialog. **Bars appear as their devices answer**, the spinner still turning, so a reader may take the first they recognise. Nothing is fetched until the dialog returns: the reader agrees to a *bar*, and its contents are read on the form, as a file's are. A dialog, not a screen, since the wire carries three fields per bar and no more ([ADR 22](adr/22-a-bar-travels-behind-one-seam.md)): a name is not a screenful.
- **The device tells two bars of one name apart.** The wire carries an id, but readers never see one and names are labels, not identity (FR-BAR-1). The list runs by device, then by bar, so one device's bars stand together unheaded. Two owned bars of one name on one device is refused where it is made (FR-BAR-1).
- **Nothing nearby says why, and offers another look in Select's place.** Both sides must be on the network, the other reader sharing and their app open: what a reader can act on, and what the [Sharing](#sharing) room warns the owner of. **Try again** stands where Select would; with nothing to take, a dead button would be clutter.
- **What arrives nearby reads exactly as a picked file does**: the same Contents cards, the **Owned | Guest** choice (**Replace | Guest** on the import entry), Save, and the owner's name to start from. **Guest keeps the source it came from**, so the bar refreshes from that device (FR-BAR-5); Owned keeps none, being a copy. A source that cannot be reached leaves the form as it stood and says why.
- **A file picked in is shown before it is agreed to**: the cards, counts and refusals of [Import](#data), the same screen. A file that will not read shows its issues and offers no road, since founding an empty bar would misstate what became of it.
- **The road is a segmented choice under "Mode", shown only once a file is in hand**. It sits above the counts, so the form ends on **Contents**, the one part read rather than answered. **Owned** is a copy, this device's to edit, with nothing linking back (FR-BAR-2, "created from a file"). **Replace** is that road onto an existing bar (FR-DAT-3). **Guest** is the owner's, read-only, refreshed from the source it arrived by (FR-BAR-3/5/7): a file sent again, or the device it was found on, hence "its source". **Guest is what an arriving bar lands on** when founding: a bar someone else shared usually stays theirs, and only this road keeps the source; a reader wanting a copy picks Owned. **On the import entry it lands on Replace**, the road for a reader who came from the open bar's gear, with a copy kept (FR-DAT-3). A file that will not read lands on no road.
- **"Mode" is the one word the question is asked in**, here and on the chip the bar list reads it back under (FR-BAR-3): one word keeps one choice from looking like two. Only the segments differ by entry (Owned or Replace), since the road does.
- **One line under it says what the chosen road will do, and only that**: a copy to edit here that nothing refreshes; everything this bar holds replaced, with a copy kept; or the owner's copy read-only and refreshed from their file.
- **The name is the reader's on every road**, the guest one included: as the unit it reads in is (FR-BAR-3), what a bar is called on this device is theirs, and no refresh takes it back. Nothing is dimmed for the name, and the road no longer decides who names the bar.
- **An owned bar's name is refused where it clashes** (FR-BAR-1): the message sits under the field and Save dims. Only another owned bar's name counts, never the bar a Replace is filling; the guest road is exempt, since a guest bar's name labels someone else's collection. So one name can dim Save on **Owned** and leave it lit on **Guest**. It is refused here because an accident made here reaches a guest's picker as two identical cards.
- **A name the road suggests gives way; a name the reader typed stands.** The file's name (ADR 21) beats an empty field, and the bar being replaced beats the file's name, since an import is not a rename. The suggestion follows the toggle until the reader types over it. Clearing the file takes back the name that came with it.

## Searchable lists

`SearchField` pinned above (NFR-1). `matchesQuery`: case-insensitive, anywhere, no surrounding space. It applies identically to ingredients (FR-ING-1) and recipes (FR-DIS-2). An entry answering several names (FR-VOC-6) matches on any and shows under its own name, so a recipe answers to its ingredients' spellings too; hence "answers to", not "is called", when nothing matches. Filter and order are widget state: never persisted, never on the collection's side.

**A narrowing changed is read from the top**: a search typed, a tag or base picked, another order chosen. A different list then stands at its first row. A collection change underneath (a rename, a stamp, an entry gone) moves nothing; only a pick is the reader's own act. It also re-anchors the scroller ([ADR 13](adr/13-lists-scroll-by-index.md)), keeping a narrowed list off the wider offset, where it would land mid-results and jerk when an unrelated card expands.

Orders sit behind one icon inside the search field, after the clear button (FR-DIS-8): tapping it brings the offered orders into the chip row as chips, ahead of the filter's own (on the recipes, before the base chip) — one scroller, no row of its own. They wear the neutral swatch, ringed with a direction arrow for the order in force. Opening or shutting them starts the row over at its first chip. Picking the current order reverses the list. No separate Z→A or missing-first chips. Ties break by name. Rows stay stable during edit; after an external change the list stays out of order until touched.

A list may also offer a **draw** over the rows on show: a button answering with the name of a row to put on screen. Only the recipes offer one (FR-DIS-5). It scrolls to the row by index ([ADR 13](adr/13-lists-scroll-by-index.md)). The draw belongs to the list, not the screen: no narrowing is named twice, and the screen learns nothing about where a row stands.

## Vocabulary editing

Ingredients and both tag tabs share one list and one pair of dialogs: search, sort orders, three faces, add button, an entry dialog and a delete dialog. The contract (fields, validation wiring, the blocked-vs-free delete split) lives in the widgets' dartdoc: `entry_list.dart`, `entry_dialog.dart`, `confirm_dialog.dart`.

Add via `FloatingActionButton`. Empty state prefills query (search miss → one tap). A successful add clears search. Recipes add pushes [recipe form](#recipe-form).

## Recipes screen

- **Card in-place expansion** (single tap, NFR-1): compact shows name, tags as dots, and ingredients ellipsized (`·`-joined) in one slot; expanded shows tags as chips, lines as `formatRecipeLine` writes, and notes, empty sections absent. Two open side by side, scroll stable. Edit and delete sit behind ⋮, beside the availability chip; delete confirms, rename keeps the card open.
- **Filter row** under search: the base chip, then recipe tags as chips, exactly the ingredients' row (FR-DIS-3). `tagFilter` takes the base chip as its leading filter, so one scroller carries both and one message joins their reasons. Narrows to recipes wearing *all* picked tags; combines with search. Add clears every pick. Availability filters through the order, not a chip.
- **Base chip** (FR-DIS-4, [ADR 12](adr/12-base-spirit-narrows.md)): reads `Base: Any`, `Base: Gin` or `Base: None`. Naming the dimension stops it passing for a tag called "Base"; the ingredient keeps its vocabulary spelling. Tapping opens **Any base**, **No base**, then every base spirit A→Z, the one in force ticked. It takes `neutralSwatch`, as an ingredient name is neither tag nor signal; the arrow marks a menu. Ringed like a picked tag while it narrows (**Any base** alone unringed), otherwise ringed transparent, so its height and spacing match a tag chip (`chipRadius` rounds both alike). Absent where nothing marks a base. A stale pick opens the list rather than emptying it, as the tag row does.
- **Random pick** (FR-DIS-5): a pair of dice above the add button, the same size so neither reads as the lesser reach. It is Font Awesome's `dice`: a roll reads as dice thrown, and the shipped single dice read as domino tiles at this size (ADR 14). It draws one recipe makeable from what is on show (Ready or Low), so the search and picks already apply; it shuts every open card and opens and scrolls to the drawn one. A second roll moves off the card standing, while another can be made, so rolling always answers. Absent where nothing is on show; rows on show but none makeable get a snackbar. The drawn card's fill washes from `secondaryContainer` to rest over 700ms: colour alone, since a height change would fire the measurement the reveal waits on (ADR 13). It runs once, so a row scrolled away and back does not repeat it.
- **Scale & convert** behind ⋮ (expanded cards only, FR-REC-7): factor ×1–×4 and one of the three fixed units, for this card alone (FR-SET-1). Display-only, dying with the card: "(×2, ml)" on the name row, measures in italic. Every card rests at ×1 in the unit the settings name ([ADR 17](adr/17-the-fixed-units-interconvert.md)); picking that unit again cancels, so under an ml reader "(part)" marks a card read otherwise. The availability chip ("Ready", "Low" or "Missing", FR-DIS-1) is the traffic light, without a count, in list order (FR-DIS-8). A low or out line carries a stock dot whose tooltip names the level; optional lines are dotted too.
- **Each ingredient a line names reaches the Ingredients screen** (FR-DIS-9). The name alone is the target, so a substitution group offers one per alternative; a whole-line tap could name only the first. The measure, "or" and "(optional)" answer nothing: part of a line responds, the cost of naming a *row*.
- **Substitution groups** (FR-REC-9, [ADR 11](adr/11-substitutions-on-the-line.md)): read as prose, "cognac or vodka", open and shut alike, where the file writes `/`. Ingredients the bar lacks fall to `dimmedInk`, but only while the group holds one; a group short of everything dims nothing and takes the dot, so the dot keeps meaning "this line is the problem". The dot follows the group's best (`stockOfLine`). The open card's "or" is italic, since two letters between two names need it; the scaled measure is italic too, and position tells them apart: the name row's "(×2, ml)" is what announces a transformed card.

## Recipe form

Create and edit: a pushed page, Save in the app bar, mirroring file order: name, lines, tags, notes.

- **Line fields** (FR-REC-2/3/8/9): grammar as in the file ("1.5 part gin (base)", "1 part gin / vodka"). `tryParseRecipeLine` runs on change, its problem under the field. Forgiving: unit omittable, plurals accepted, case and aliases resolved, `/` spaced or not. The form saves as typed, and the line lands under the vocabulary's spelling. Fields show `/`, cards show "or": the form is where a line is re-edited.
- **Self-growing lines**: an empty line sits at the bottom; typing adds the next, erasing a spare removes it. Empty lines drop on save. No drag or delete button.
- **Save blocked** until the name passes live checks and every line parses. `validateRecipe` checks the whole on save: per-field issues under their field, list-level refusals as a snackbar.
- **Unknown ingredients**: if that is the only issue, confirm to add them (out, untagged). Aliases resolve first, so no near-duplicates; a group offers only alternatives no ingredient answers to.
- **Tags**: chip picker (recipe vocab). **Notes**: multiline, opens one line tall.
- **Back**: untouched pops silently; dirty asks to discard. The whole entry is one disk save.

## Ingredients screen

- **Opens on stock (fullest first)** (FR-DIS-8), one row per ingredient, stable during edit. The stock chip (green, amber or red; words, not a hue to decode) cycles `in → low → out → in` on a row tap (FR-ING-2).
- **Edit/delete** behind ⋮. Edit dialog: name, aliases, tags; the whole entry saves at once. A new ingredient starts out. Tags show as dots after the name, in vocabulary order, in the legend's colour.
- **Filter row** (legend): ingredient tags as chips (horizontal scroll). Picking narrows to ingredients with *all* picked tags (combines with name search, FR-ING-3). Add clears picks.
- **Three faces**: empty, no match, list (third names narrowing source).

## Shopping screen

- **Budget picks exactly N** (FR-DIS-6): `SegmentedButton` 1/2/3 reads baskets of *that* many ingredients, not up to that many. The best few of each size come off one search ([ADR 15](adr/15-the-optimizer-answers-with-the-best-few.md)), so a one-ingredient win is never buried under the three-ingredient baskets that almost always unlock more.
- **One search, not three**: the screen searches once at the largest budget and reads each size off the answer, so moving between sizes costs nothing ([components.md](components.md#computations)).
- **"Low too" switch** (FR-DIS-7, [ADR 16](adr/16-the-optimizer-buys-what-is-running-low.md)): sets what counts as short. Off, only what is out of stock; on, anything short of full stock, so low ingredients are bought beside missing ones. The label is two words, since readers scan labels; the sentence sits in the tooltip.
- **The tags say what to shop for** (FR-DIS-10): the [recipes screen](#recipes-screen)'s chip row, the same `tagFilter`, on its own row under the controls. The budget and switch say how much; the chips say what for. Always shown, not behind an icon: a narrowing that gaps the numbering must read beside the gap.
- **A chip is read one of two ways** ([Shopping settings](#shopping) picks which, [ADR 24](adr/24-the-tags-may-aim-the-optimizer.md)); the numbering says which is in force.
  - **Sifting**, the default: the search runs over everything and the chips keep what answers them. A basket answers to the tags of every recipe it unlocks. A basket is ranked among all of its size, so a narrowed screen reads `#1, #4, #7`: the number *is* the ranking, and renumbering would call the fourth-best basket the best. The gap says a chip is doing something. The cost is [ADR 15](adr/15-the-optimizer-answers-with-the-best-few.md)'s: a basket already cut for ranking poorly overall never reaches the chips, so a niche tag can read empty while a good basket exists. **Baskets** on the settings screen is the relief.
  - **Aiming**: the picks go *into* the search, which weighs a basket by how many of the recipes it unlocks wear any pick. A basket scoring nothing is dropped by the rule that already drops a zero yield, so every basket shown answers the picks and the numbering runs unbroken. It costs no more than sifting: the pool is the gaps' own ingredients either way; only the weighing moves.
- **The card is the same under both.** A basket still names and counts every recipe it closes. Aiming changes which baskets show and where they rank, never what a basket says of itself; tagged recipes gather at the top, wearing the dots they already wore.
- **The picks re-key the search only while aiming.** Sifting must not re-run the one expensive computation on a chip tap, so tags stay out of the provider's key there and go in here ([components.md](components.md#state-contracts)).
- **Both controls on one row** above the list, in a `Wrap` rather than a `Row`: on a narrow phone or with large text, the switch drops to a second line rather than running off the edge. Segments sit at 48dp, a touch target's floor; `visualDensity` reaches them, as `minimumSize` is dropped on the way to a segment.
- **Cards expand in place**, as on the [recipes screen](#recipes-screen). The title is `Shopping Cart #N`, `#1` being the best at the size in force; the subtitle is the ingredients, `+`-joined and clipped, with the count trailing in `onSurfaceVariant`. A number is no signal, so there is no chip. The headline being the rank, the count beside it reads as ranking, not as a name no two of which are the same length.
- **A basket is its ingredients** (FR-DIS-6) **and the rank is only where it stands**: an open card is remembered under the ingredients, so moving the budget away and back finds it open, while `#1` at another size is another, shut basket.
- **Nothing is named twice.** Open, the subtitle goes and the ingredients read in the body, labelled `Ingredients` and each at the level it stands, beside `Unlocks` and every recipe in full: two labelled bullet runs, as on the [import review](#data), via the same `BulletRuns` widget. `Buy` is refused as the label, since the controls row above already spends that word on the budget.
- **An open basket marks the picks it answered** (FR-DIS-10): each recipe under `Unlocks` wears, as dots in vocabulary order, the picked tags it carries. `TagDots` draws the same run a list row wears, so a dot and its chip share a colour. Only picked tags show: a dot answers the one question a narrowed screen raises, and nothing is marked while nothing narrows. Other tags are refused: on the recipe list the dots are the row's own subject, but on a basket they would bury the answer.
- **Every name on an open basket leads somewhere** (FR-DIS-9): an ingredient to the Ingredients screen, a recipe to the Recipes, one tap either way. The card stays open and keeps its budget, so back lands on the basket the reader left.
- **The stock dots are what the switch is worth reading**: restocking mixes ingredients merely running low with ones none of which is left, and the open card is where that is told. Plain, they all read out, which is the same thing a recipe card says about a line it is short of.
- **Three empty states**, told apart by why there is nothing: no recipes to be short of; nothing short at all (worded by the reading in force); or nothing on show. Only the last has somewhere to go: it offers the smallest size that answers *under the tags in force* and moves there, and offers nothing where no size does. With a tag picked it blames the picks, joining its reasons as `_NoMatch` does on the other lists, since the size did not empty the screen.
- **Off screen it does not search.** While another destination is on show this screen watches nothing; the optimizer must not run for a reader who is not there ([components.md](components.md#state-contracts)).

## Tags screen

Behind Settings. One tab per vocabulary (Recipe, Ingredient, FR-VOC-4). A tag draws as a chip, its name on the full-strength colour. Opens A→Z; palette order is also offered (FR-DIS-8).

- **Row tap** opens edit: name and colour together, one save. Neither tab knows its kind; the domain tells via `TagKind`.
- **A guest bar reads it** (FR-BAR-4): search, tabs and orders stand; the add button, the ⋮ and the edit tap go. The empty state drops its description of what a tag is for: an invitation with nothing to take up.

## Units

Behind Settings. A form, not `EntryCardList` (no search or sort). Rows editable in place (name, plural); one Save for the screen. Rename propagates to lines.

- **Self-growing**: an empty row sits at the bottom; typing adds the next, erasing a spare removes it.
- **`part`/`ml`/`oz` locked** (the ratios and the global unit are anchored, ADR 09/17); plurals stay editable.
- **Delete blocked** while lines use it (names recipes). An unused row goes on save; discard restores.
- **Validation** as on import: errors under the field, Save blocked.
- **A guest bar reads the rows** (FR-BAR-4): every field goes quiet, and the spare row, deletes and Save go too: an invitation, three refusals and a button that never lights. The lock goes as well, as it marks the three names nobody may rename, which every row would wear. `EditorScaffold` takes `readOnly`, not `onSave: null`: nothing valid to save *yet* dims the button rather than dropping it.

## Amounts

Behind Settings, below [Units](#units): what those three fixed names are worth (FR-SET-1). It shares the Units screen's shape and `EditorScaffold`, so Save and the discard prompt read alike: one dimmed sentence, the global unit, two rows. Nothing else.

- **The bar's unit**: a `SegmentedButton` over the fixed three, the recipe card's own picker, since it is one choice for the whole bar. One Save, two writes: the sizes go to the collection as the owner's, the pick to the bar's record as the reader's ([ADR 21](adr/21-the-file-carries-one-bar.md)), so two bars may read in different units.
- **A guest bar reads the rows and moves the pick** (FR-BAR-3). The unit read in is the reader's preference; what a part and an ounce are worth is the owner's, as the recipes were written against them. So both show, the fields go quiet, and Save writes the record alone. The rows show no error: nothing typed there can be wrong, and the owner's sizes are not the reader's to fault.
- **Two rows, a sentence each**: "1 part = [30] ml", the number their only field. The global unit leads, *except* under ml, where "1 ml = 0.0333 part" is a number no one reads back; there each row leads with the unit it sizes, the pair the file stores, so the pick is a plain view of storage. The three parts are columns, not a written width, so fields stay in line whatever the unit spellings, and larger text widens the sentence rather than wrapping it.
- **A row owns one size**: the first is what a part is worth, the second what an ounce is. Redefining the part leaves the ounce where it stood ([ADR 17](adr/17-the-fixed-units-interconvert.md): a part is a preference, an ounce a constant), and the row reading across both follows. A pick rewrites both readings and no size, so cycling the three cannot drift them.
- **Readings round to four decimals**, enough to spell a US ounce exactly. Only the reading rounds: a row left alone writes nothing back, so a stored size keeps every digit.
- **Validation** as on import, plus: a ratio must be a number above zero, zero having no inverse to store.

## Shopping

Behind Settings, below [Amounts](#amounts): what the [shopping screen](#shopping-screen) is asked and where it opens (FR-SET-2, [ADR 24](adr/24-the-tags-may-aim-the-optimizer.md)). The one row that dims on a guest bar, the optimizer being absent there, not empty (FR-BAR-4).

- **No Save, and no `EditorScaffold`**: the one thing telling it from the two screens above. Every control is a fixed pick or a switch, so nothing needs validating or discarding, and a Save would be a second tap. Each control settles on the tap and writes to the bar's record.
- **Five controls, which is what makes it a room.** Two rows are not a room, so [Data](#data)'s two rows stay on the Settings list. The five: how a tag pick reads, how many baskets a size offers, the budget, the reading of short, and the optional lines.
- **A section label, a control, a note**: the [bar form](#new-bar)'s shape. `SectionLabel` names the question, the control answers it, and a `FieldNote` says in one line what the answer does. The tag note changes with the pick, each reading getting its own line.
- **A switch reads as one of them**: its label at the section labels' weight, on the same line as the switch, so the five read as five answers rather than two kinds of control.
- **Two of them are where the screen starts, not what it is.** The budget and "low too" seed the shopping screen's controls, which then move freely and write nothing back. A change here takes hold at once: the shell keeps that screen alive across a crossing, so a fresh one would leave the change unread until the reader left the bar.

## Data

Two rows on the Settings list, below [Shopping](#shopping): **Share**, which opens [the room](#sharing) every way a bar leaves is in; and the way back in, **Import**, or **Refresh** on a guest bar ([ADR 18](adr/18-data-crosses-the-edge-in-a-system-sheet.md)). Import travels to the form every way in is on, so its row carries a chevron. It once opened the system picker directly, leaving a file the only road in and a nearby device out of reach of an open bar.

- **A row that acts carries no chevron**; one that travels does. That is the whole telling, and why both kinds may share the Settings list. `_Entry` has two constructors rather than a flag, so a row's shape is fixed where it is written.
- **Every caption is one line.** Wrapped captions read as twelve rows, not six, and broke where the phone's width landed, not where the sense did. Captions stay: **Units** and **Amounts** are the pair no title tells apart (one is the vocabulary, the other what a part and an ounce are worth).
- **Import** (FR-DAT-3, FR-BAR-8) opens the [New bar](#new-bar) form on nothing in hand, with the roads **From import | From LAN** it is founded by; the system picker or nearby dialog is the next tap. That costs one tap more than a picker-first row, and buys the road it lacked: replacing the open bar from a nearby device as from a file. It is the *same* screen a bar is founded on, since two screens asking the same questions would drift.
- **The contents stand in for the file's name**, and open. Recipes, ingredients, tags and units, recipes first: a reader tells files apart by their recipes long before their units. Android gives no usable filename ([ADR 18](adr/18-data-crosses-the-edge-in-a-system-sheet.md)), and the collection is what is agreed to anyway. One card per kind: count as title, names as the line beneath, cut off at one line; tapped, it gives every name it counted. **Nothing is cut short**: a list stopping at fifty would hide the entry a reader came for.
- **Each kind is named and ordered as its managing screen does**: the nouns and A→Z of `ingredients_screen.dart` and `tags_screen.dart`, and the declared order `units_screen.dart` leaves, the fixed three first ([ADR 17](adr/17-the-fixed-units-interconvert.md)). The two tag vocabularies share one count but keep separate labelled runs, since one name may stand in both ([ADR 07](adr/07-tag-colour.md)). An empty run is left out rather than heading nothing; a kind the file lacks offers no chevron and answers no tap.
- **The cards run to the width of the fields above them.** `EntryCard` insets itself for the lists it was written for, which on this already-padded form left counts narrower than the name field and segments for no reason. The row takes its margin as an argument, defaulted to the lists' own, so the inset is still set in one place.
- **The roads are a segmented choice, and Save is the commit**, the form's own shape (`editor_form.dart`). **Replace** puts what arrived in place of everything the open bar holds, with a copy kept first (FR-DAT-3); **Guest** leaves that bar alone and founds one beside it (FR-BAR-7). A road chosen, then agreed to, is one question at a time, as in founding; two app-bar commits would read as a dialog wearing a page. Under the choice sits what the counts cannot say: what the road will do, and to which bar by name. No numbers there: the cards have them.
- **Import is reached from an owned bar alone**: on a guest bar whose collection is not this device's to replace, the row reads **Refresh** (FR-BAR-4, FR-BAR-5), so Replace is offered without checking whose bar is shown. A device holding only guest bars reaches the guest road through [New bar](#new-bar), where an arriving bar belongs as a new bar, not an edit.
- **A refused file offers nothing to agree to.** Its issues read as the startup banner words them, `line N:` and all (FR-DAT-4), under one sentence: nothing has changed. A file the app cannot read holds nothing to import, so there is no button to grey out.
- **Either road leaves for the collection**, popping Settings with the form: what arrived is two screens back, and a new list says more than a sentence. **Only Replace says what landed**: it returns the reader to where they started, with only the contents changed. The two that found a bar land the reader *in* a bar that was not there a moment ago, which no sentence improves on. An import can speak at all, unlike an export, whose outcome a share cannot know.
- **Only a failure speaks otherwise**: a picker that will not open, a file that cannot be read, a road that cannot be written. A reader who picks nothing has done nothing, and a refused file has the screen to say so. A road that failed stays on the form: leaving for a collection that never reached the disk would misreport it.
- **Refresh, in the same row's place on a guest bar** (FR-BAR-5), asks the source again: the pull's question, where a reader looking for it will find it, and the only way to ask on a bar too short to pull. **Its caption names the source**, **Pick a newer file** or **Ask the sharing device again**, since a guest refreshes only from the way it was added. It **says what it came to right there**: a failed refresh's banner sits behind this screen unread, so the answer comes as a snackbar, marked told, and the banner does not repeat it on the way back. A successful refresh says so too; the reader is looking at a menu, not the lists.
- **The row turns while the source is being reached.** The ask outlasts the tap by as long as the network takes, so the spinner replaces the row's icon and no second tap lands until it does. Work is shown on what started it, as the [New bar](#new-bar) wait is.
- **A source that did not answer says so in one line**: *Could not refresh: source not found.* The bar standing as it was is already in view, so naming it again says nothing actionable. A file's refusal keeps its length: those are issues to read, not a fact to take in.

## Sharing

A pushed screen behind Settings, on the **Share** row above [Data](#data)'s **Import** and **Change bar**: every way a bar leaves, file and LAN in one room (FR-BAR-6). Out comes before in, and the room sits above the way out, being about a bar rather than which bar.

- **One room, two doors.** Settings opens it on the bar in hand; a bar card's ⋮ opens it on any bar ([Bars](#bars)), so a reader can share without crossing into the bar. Each bar is shared separately (FR-BAR-6), so the screen takes a bar rather than assuming the open one. It is a screen, not a dialog, because a failure speaks in a snackbar, which a modal barrier would dim and make untappable: the objection [Data](#data) raises to speaking after a system sheet.
- **Every way out in one room.** A file is an act and the LAN an arrangement, but both concern the same bar, so a reader who came to hand it over finds both here. Export and Share were two Settings rows, and a file row in the room was refused for duplicating Export; merging the rows let it in. A way whose adapter this build lacks is absent, not dimmed, as on the bottom bar. The file way is always there, which is why the Settings row never goes.
- **What the bar is called leads the screen**: the bar's own name, the record behind its card, the title above, the file's name, and a guest's browsed entry ([ADR 21](adr/21-the-file-carries-one-bar.md)). Not a sharing name: two names for one bar would drift, so renaming here is the bars list's rename. A guest's name is a label on this device, exempt from the owned rule (FR-BAR-1/3); an owned one is refused under the field when it clashes, as on the [new-bar form](#new-bar). The button below keeps the gap the [Import](#new-bar) form leaves. **It is never locked**: a bar on offer is listed again under its new name with its path unchanged ([ADR 22](adr/22-a-bar-travels-behind-one-seam.md)), so nothing strands. The device's name differs, since a guest's source keeps it.
- **The file way is one button**, **Export as file**: the tonal full-width button, under no heading, as the button says what it does. It opens the system share sheet on a copy of the bar (FR-DAT-1), a guest's as an owned bar's. **Nothing is said on success**: the opening sheet is the answer, and a snackbar would land under the modal and read stale. A failed write or a sheet that will not open speaks; dismissing the chooser is no failure.
- **An empty collection exports anyway.** A file with `format: 1` and empty sections is a template, not an error, and the screen need not know which it is. It imports on the same terms, reading as the nothing it holds.
- **What this device is called opens the LAN block**: the name a guest reads in their own picker ([ADR 28](adr/28-the-device-is-named-by-its-reader.md)). It sits above the switch because it is announced the moment the switch goes on. It arrives filled with the reader's own phone name, so no prompt is needed: a prompt would charge every reader for the few who care.
- **Leaving a field is the commit**, as every other Settings control acts where it stands. Both name fields are one widget (`CommittedField`). A field only looked at renames nothing: the phone's own name is what the device announces anyway, and writing it down would freeze it against later change. A field left blank, or refused by the name rule, gives back the name that stood: a nameless device is no better off than one named by its phone.
- **The device name is read-only while this device is announcing anything.** The announcement went up under the old name and a guest's source keeps it, so a mid-offer rename would silently strand every guest. The lock is device-wide, as the announcement is. On a bar whose own switch is off, the line under the field names the bar holding it, since a control dimmed for an unshown reason looks broken.
- **What the network granted is not shown.** A clash is settled by suffix, `Nikita's phone (2)`, and the room says nothing about it ([ADR 27](adr/27-nearby-comes-off-bonsoir.md)): the suffix belongs to one announcement and outlives nothing; an explaining line would reach every reader for the few whose name collided.
- **Over the LAN, a switch**: on is an offer, off a withdrawal. While a change is going out there is no switch, only a progress mark: the reader has already moved it, and a switch sliding back would misreport what is happening.
- **The room names its bar in its own title**, `Share "Home bar"`, so the ⋮ opening it on a bar not in hand needs no other way of saying which. The **LAN device name** field carries nothing beneath it: its purpose needs no saying, and the only line it shows explains why it refuses a change.
- **The one line under the switch says sharing works only until the app is closed.** It is the fact a reader is most likely to be caught by, as when a guest asks while the owner's phone is in a pocket. It is now literally true of the record too: an offer does not survive the app, so the switch is off next time and re-sharing is one tap. Nothing else is said there: that withdrawing is no recall would tell a reader turning sharing off about a guest's copy, when they meant their own bar.
- **A failure speaks once and leaves nothing behind.** The record moves first and the network second ([ADR 22](adr/22-a-bar-travels-behind-one-seam.md)), so the switch tells the truth either way; the snackbar says what the switch cannot: shared but not announced, or withdrawn without the network being told. Nothing stays in the room: a standing line would need the app to know what is announced now, which a failed announcement cannot say.
- **A guest bar has the same room**, offered over the LAN as an owned bar is: a guest already hands the collection over as a file, and the two ways are not worth refusing differently (FR-BAR-4, FR-BAR-6). A second guest reads the first's copy as of its last refresh; withdrawing it ends their refreshes and nothing else. Where a guest refreshes *from* is fixed, the way it was added (FR-BAR-5), and the room does not change that: a reader wanting another way adds the bar again by it.

## Tag and stock colours

Each token is a fill and ink pair per theme, in `palette.dart`. One traffic light covers stock, availability and whose bar it is (FR-BAR-3): green where the app is fully open to the reader, amber where it is not, red where nothing can be made. Fill is the chip or dot colour; ink is the text or pick ring. `switch` is exhaustive ([ADR 07](adr/07-tag-colour.md)).

`neutralSwatch` is the exception and the reason the rest are fixed: built from scheme roles (`surfaceContainerHighest`/`onSurfaceVariant`), it reads as neither a tag colour nor a signal, and follows light and dark for free. It is worn by a chip whose colour means nothing: today, the base filter.
